# VOICE — Nevyn Application Writing

This file should evolve from real Nevyn-authored samples. Do not manufacture quirks merely to appear human.

## Default voice

- Direct and technically literate.
- Specific before impressive.
- Comfortable naming tools, architecture, data, and decisions when relevant.
- Concise; avoids ceremonial application language.
- Confident without claiming expertise that the evidence does not support.
- Uses ordinary vocabulary where a technical term is not needed.
- Explains why an experience transfers to the employer's problem.

## Preferred structure

For experience answers:

`problem/context -> what I did -> technical detail/decision -> result/evidence -> relevance`

For short questions, answer the question in sentence one.

## Avoid

- "I am writing to express my interest"
- "I am thrilled/excited to apply"
- "I am passionate about"
- "unique blend"
- "perfectly aligns"
- "leveraging" when "using" works
- "cutting-edge"
- "dynamic"
- "innovative" without evidence
- "fast-paced environment"
- "proven track record"
- "I believe I would be an excellent fit"
- generic praise of the company
- repeating the JD's marketing language
- excessive em dashes
- repetitive three-item rhetorical lists
- inflated adjectives
- grand concluding paragraphs
- fake anecdotes/hooks
- symmetrical paragraphs that read like a template

## Rhythm

- Mix short declarative sentences with longer technical explanations.
- Contractions are fine when natural.
- Prefer active voice, but do not mechanically eliminate passive voice when it is clearer.
- Do not force every sentence into the same length or cadence.

## Company specificity test

Any sentence describing why the company/role is interesting should fail this test:

> Could this exact sentence be pasted into 100 other applications?

If yes, replace it with something tied to the company's actual product, engineering problem, customers, architecture, market, or role responsibilities.
